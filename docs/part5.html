From numbers to words to action

Generative AI can write a fluent report in seconds, but a fluent text is not necessarily a true one. This part therefore keeps one rule throughout: The numbers come from the analysis, the words may come from the AI, and the judgment stays with the team. The four stations practice this rule, from catching the errors of a generated text and building a safe prompt to writing a proposal for the NOKTA workshop.

Spot the errors

An AI assistant was asked to summarize the results of Campus B, and it wrote the nine sentences below. Some of them are correct, while others contain an invented number, a wrong comparison, a cause that the data cannot show or a source that does not exist. Click every sentence you would not sign with your own name, then press Check the marks to see an explanation for each sentence.

The facts it should have used

PointPM2.5Δref°C Δref

For each point of Campus B, the table gives the two-round mean of PM2.5 in µg/m³ and its difference from the quiet reference point. The last column gives the difference in temperature from the reference in °C. These are the only numbers that the assistant was allowed to use.

Takeaway. Language models can produce fluent text that is not supported by their input, which is often called hallucination [1]. Every number in a generated text is therefore checked against the analysis table, and every cause, comparison or source against the study design and the reference list.
Colab

Section 5.3 of the Colab notebook contains a number auditor. It reads a text, lists every number in it and marks each number that does not appear in the fact table, allowing only for rounding. Exercise 5.A asks how many numbers of a sample text the auditor flags.

2

A safe prompt

A good prompt does not ask the assistant to know the numbers. It hands the numbers over, names the audience and writes the rules down. The builder below fills in the fact table of the chosen campus directly from the analysis and adds the study design and seven rules. The finished prompt can be copied into any assistant.

Use the assistant that your institution allows, and never paste personal data into it. Whatever the assistant writes goes back through the number auditor of the notebook and through the claim check of the next station before it is used.

Colab

Sections 5.1 and 5.2 of the Colab notebook build the same fact table and the same prompt from the data loaded in Section 0. A team that loads its own NOKTA files therefore gets a prompt with its own numbers.

How strong is the claim?

Field measurements at six points show what was there and what went together. They rarely prove what caused what, because nothing was changed on purpose. Match each of the four sentences below to the strongest wording that the NOKTA design supports, then press Check.

4

The solution card

On 28 November the NOKTA National Environment Workshop works at three thematic tables. Every proposal brought there has to answer five questions: The problem, the evidence, the solution, its feasibility and the role of young people. The card below starts from a finding of the analysis and fills in an example answer for each question, which the team can then rewrite in its own words.

The card is kept only in this browser and appears again in the debrief of the NOKTA Lab, where it can be printed. The example findings come from the synthetic data. With real NOKTA data the team starts from its own finding and writes its own card. When the team can go out, the Field practice tab of the NOKTA Lab gives the card a first check on site.

Colab

Section 5.4 of the Colab notebook prints the same card as text, starting from a finding of the team's own fact table.
