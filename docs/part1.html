Part 1 · Measure · 0:08 to 0:30

The campus as data

No AI method can produce better results than the data it receives, so the readings must first be understood and checked. This part replays one field round of the station and explains what each of the ten channels measures and how it can fail. It then cleans the readings step by step. Every reading that is removed or corrected stays visible, so the cleaning itself can be checked as well.

In short. The measuring box does not always measure correctly. After each move it needs about two minutes to adjust to the new place. Sometimes a sensor gets stuck and keeps writing the same value, sometimes it produces a single absurd value, and sometimes it records nothing. In humid air the dust sensor shows more dust than there is, and the three boxes measure slightly differently from each other. This part shows how these errors are found and corrected. The main idea: A measured number is not trusted at once, it is checked first.

Ten channels, one reading

Each row of a NOKTA file describes one moment at one point: A time stamp, the campus, the round, the point and the ten numbers measured at that moment. The units matter more than they seem. A PM2.5 value of 35 µg/m³ means one thing as a 10-second reading and something quite different as a 24-hour mean. A value without its unit and its averaging time therefore cannot be interpreted. Air pressure is about 900 hPa on Campus A and about 1015 hPa on Campus C. What explains most of this gap?
Air pressure falls with height, because a higher place has less air above it. Campus A lies about 1000 m above sea level and Campus C at the coast, so most of the gap of about 115 hPa comes from altitude. Pressure differences between campuses therefore describe their location, not their air quality.

A robust test for spikes

The usual z-score measures how far a reading lies from the mean, in units of the standard deviation. A large spike, however, pulls both the mean and the standard deviation towards itself, and in this way it hides its own size. The modified z-score replaces them with the median and the median absolute deviation (MAD), which a single spike cannot move [1].

Mi​=MAD0.6745(xi​−x~)​MAD=medianj​∣xj​−x~∣​

xi one reading (µg/m³) · x̃ median of the readings · MAD median of the distances to x̃ · the factor 0.6745 makes M comparable with an ordinary z-score. A reading with |M| above about 3 deserves a closer look. The detector in the notebook uses the stricter limit of 6 together with the physical rules below.

Worked example. Seven PM2.5 readings: 14.2, 15.0, 13.8, 14.6, 97.3, 15.1, 14.4. Their median is 14.6, and the distances to the median are 0.4, 0.4, 0.8, 0.0, 82.7, 0.5 and 0.2, so MAD = 0.4. For the reading 97.3 the modified score is M = 0.6745 × 82.7 / 0.4 = 139.45. This is far above 3, so the spike is flagged. The ordinary z-score uses the mean 26.34 and the standard deviation 28.97, and gives z = (97.3 − 26.34) / 28.97 = 2.45. This value stays below 3, so with the ordinary score the spike hides itself.

The detector in the notebook adds physical rules to this test. A real plume of smoke or exhaust lifts PM1, PM2.5 and PM10 together, while a glitch of the sensor affects one channel alone, so only single-channel spikes are flagged. CO₂ readings of 0 or 2000 ppm lie outside the measuring range of the sensor. A CO₂ value that does not change at all for two minutes points to a frozen sensor, because real air always varies a little.

4

Three stations, one story

Before the field rounds, the three NOKTA Stations ran side by side at one place for two hours. Since they measured the same air, any steady difference between them is a property of the devices and not of the campuses. Such a difference must be removed before the readings of different campuses are analyzed together.

Station S2 reads about half a degree warmer than the others. Which station is right?
Without a calibrated reference instrument, no station can be declared right. The stations are therefore aligned to their common mean, minute by minute. This removes the differences between the devices, but it cannot remove an error that all three share, which only a reference instrument could reveal.

Humid air fools the particle counter

On the humid morning of Round 2 on Campus C, the raw PM2.5 of the green area is higher than at the road. Why?
The green area was the first stop of the round, at 87 % relative humidity. In such humid air the particles take up water and grow. A light-scattering sensor without a dryer sees these larger, wet particles and reports more mass than the dry particles really have [3, 4].
The cause is the air, not the place. The green area was the first stop of the round, at 87 % relative humidity. In such humid air the particles take up water and grow, and a light-scattering sensor without a dryer reports more mass than the dry particles really have [3, 4].
Relative humidity

C(aw​)=1+−1+1/aw​κρw​/ρp​​,aw​=100RH​PMcorrected​=CPMraw​​​

C growth factor of particle mass (no unit) · κ hygroscopicity of the particles, 0.3 as an average continental value · ρw/ρp density of water over the density of dry particles, 1/1.65 · RH relative humidity (%) [4].

Worked example. At the green area RH = 87.3 %, so aw = 0.873. The denominator is −1 + 1/0.873 = 0.145, and the numerator is 0.3/1.65 = 0.182. The growth factor is therefore C = 1 + 0.182/0.145 = 2.255. Dividing the raw median of 15.07 µg/m³ by C gives 15.07/2.255 = 6.7 µg/m³.
rawcorrected

The chart compares the raw and the corrected PM2.5 visit medians of Campus C in Round 2, in µg/m³. Before the correction, the green area, measured first in the humid morning air, looks more polluted than the point near the road. After the correction it is again the cleanest point, as its trees and its distance from the road would suggest. The value of κ belongs to the local air, so a real study does not take 0.3 as given but estimates κ by comparison with a reference instrument.

Takeaway. A low-cost sensor reports what it sees, which is not always what is there [5]. Quality control, the alignment of the devices and physical corrections therefore come before any AI method, and each step is written down so that others can check it.
