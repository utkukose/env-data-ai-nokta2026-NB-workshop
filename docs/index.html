NOKTA workshop · ÜNİDES 2026 · two hours

AI-Based Environmental Data Analysis and Interpretation

From campus sensor readings to evidence, and from evidence to action.

PM2.5Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

In the NOKTA project, student teams carry one portable measurement station to six fixed points on their campus. At each point the station records ten sensor channels for 15 minutes, and the whole route is measured in two rounds on two different days. This workshop follows such a dataset from the first raw reading to a proposal that a university unit can put into practice. Along the way it shows where artificial intelligence (AI) really helps the analysis, where it can mislead, and which checks keep the conclusions honest.

In short. Students carry a portable measuring box to six different places on a campus: A shaded passage, a roadside, a quiet lawn, a sunny concrete square, a busy entrance and a spot under trees. The box stays 15 minutes at each place and measures values such as temperature, humidity, dust, carbon dioxide and light. The same round is repeated a week later, and all of this is done on three different campuses: a campus high above sea level (A), a city campus (B) and a coastal campus (C). The data in this workshop are not real. They were generated for this scenario. Step by step, the workshop goes from these measurements to a proposal.

The 3D scene is a schematic campus, not the map of a real one. It places the six point types where they usually occur on a campus: A shaded passage between two buildings, a point next to the campus road, a quiet lawn, a sunny concrete square, a busy faculty entrance and a row of trees. The column above each point shows the mean of the two field rounds for the channel selected with the buttons above. The label gives the same value with its unit. A taller and darker column therefore means a higher value. Whatever channel the columns show, the dust cloud around each point always follows PM2.5, so the road and the busy entrance stand out before any number is read.

The two hours

The session lasts two hours and consists of five parts and a closing lab. Every part opens with a question that the participants answer before the result is shown, so that intuition can be compared with what the data say. Each result on the pages can then be reproduced in Python in the matching section of the Colab notebook.

0:08 to 0:30

1 · Measure: The campus as data

A field round is replayed in 3D, so the route of the station and the build-up of each reading can be followed point by point. The ten sensor channels are introduced with their units and typical ranges. Faulty readings such as sensor warm-up, sudden spikes and a frozen channel are then found with robust statistics. Finally, the three stations are aligned with a side-by-side test, and particle readings taken in humid air are corrected.

 0:30 to 0:52

2 · Analyze: Fair comparisons

The air warms during the morning, so the point visited last can look warmer than it really is. This part shows how the reversed order of the second round removes this effect of the clock, and why the median describes a typical minute better than the mean. It also shows how a strong correlation over all campuses together can point the wrong way inside each campus.

 0:57 to 1:17

3 · Discover: AI finds patterns

The k-means algorithm groups the 468 clean minutes in a rotating 3D space without being told where they were measured. Principal component analysis (PCA) then compresses six channels into a few new axes that keep most of the variation. At the end the participants name the groups themselves and check whether the algorithm has found real microclimates such as shade, sun and traffic.

 1:17 to 1:37

4 · Explain: Why is it high here?

A random forest model predicts PM2.5 from traffic, people, greenery, sunshine and weather. The model is first tested honestly, by keeping whole measurement points out of the training data instead of random minutes. The part then asks which inputs the model really needs. Finally, the participants try what-if scenarios with traffic and the background level and see the answer of the model as a surface in 3D.

 1:37 to 1:55

5 · Share and turn into action

An AI assistant has written a short report on Campus B, and some of its sentences are wrong. The participants first find these errors and see why each one is an error. They then build a prompt that gives the assistant only verified numbers and clear rules. Finally, they write a solution card for one of the thematic tables of the NOKTA workshop on 28 November.

 1:55 to 2:00

NOKTA Lab and debrief

The NOKTA Lab gathers the three 3D tools of the session on one page: The campus twin, the cloud of minutes and the surface of the model. It also writes a short field report from the answers and choices made in this browser during the session. The report can be printed or saved as a PDF file. A last tab offers six short field tasks for teams that can go out on a real campus.

The opening takes the first eight minutes (0:00 to 0:08). A five-minute break follows Part 2, and the key B shows a countdown for it on the screen.

The data of the session

The workshop uses a synthetic dataset that follows the NOKTA field protocol. It contains three campuses, A, B and C, and each campus has the same six point types. On each campus one NOKTA Station visits the six points in two rounds held one week apart, and the second round visits them in reverse order. Each visit lasts 15 minutes, the station takes a reading every 10 seconds, and the sensors sit about 1.5 m above the ground, at breathing height.

All values were generated by a script with a fixed seed value. They are plausible for a sunny November morning, but they describe no real campus and must not be quoted as findings. The aim of NOKTA is not to rank universities or cities, and the workshop follows the same rule. Points are compared within their own campus, and campuses are never set against each other.

Six point types

PointTypeDescription from the field form
P1Shaded, between buildingsA narrow passage between two buildings that gets no direct sun during the visit.
P2Near trafficA point two meters from the campus road, where buses and cars pass and stop.
P3Quiet referenceThe edge of a lawn far from roads and entrances, used as the calm baseline of the campus.
P4Sunny hard surfaceA concrete square in full sun, where the hard surface stores heat.
P5Busy student areaA faculty entrance with many passing students and a smoking group nearby.
P6Green areaA path under a row of trees, shaded and cooled by the leaves.

Round 1 starts at 10:00 and visits the points from P1 to P6. Round 2 takes place one week later at the same clock times and visits them from P6 back to P1. Every point is therefore measured once early and once late in the morning.

How to follow

Nothing needs to be installed. The pages run in any modern browser, including a phone, and a downloaded copy also opens without an internet connection. The orange boxes on each page open the matching section of the Colab notebook, where every result can be run again, changed and checked in Python.

Keys: ← → stations, [ ] parts, T paper or night view, L language, F full screen, B break, ? help.

Colab

A single Colab notebook carries the whole session. Its Sections 1 to 5 follow Parts 1 to 5 of the site, and Section 0 loads the data. With one change of setting, the same notebook also reads the real NOKTA files of a team.
