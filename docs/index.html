AI-Based Environmental Data Analysis and Interpretation

From campus sensor readings to evidence, and from evidence to action.

PM2.5Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

Six measurement points on a campus, one portable station, two field rounds and ten sensor channels. This workshop follows such data from the first reading to a solution proposal. It shows where artificial intelligence (AI) helps, where it misleads, and how a careful analyst keeps it honest.

The data of the session

The workshop runs on a synthetic dataset that follows the NOKTA field protocol. Three campuses (A, B and C) each have six fixed point types. One NOKTA Station per campus visits the six points in two rounds, and the second round reverses the order. Each visit lasts 15 minutes with a reading every 10 seconds, at about 1.5 m above the ground.

All values are invented by a script with a fixed seed. They are plausible for a sunny November morning but describe no real campus, so they must not be quoted as findings. The aim of NOKTA is not to rank universities or cities, and the workshop follows the same rule.
